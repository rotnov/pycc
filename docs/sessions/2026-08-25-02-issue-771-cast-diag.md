## What changed

`ConstraintEnvironment` (`crates/pycc_types/src/constraints.rs`) gains
a third binding set, `opaque_bindings`, alongside `bindings` and
`maybe_bindings`. An unconditional assignment whose initializer has no
solver-representable term (`cast` to a class, `.pop()`, an attribute
read, a method call, a heterogeneous container literal) now marks its
target as definitely-but-opaquely bound instead of leaving it
untracked, so `HirExpr::Name`'s lookup returns "no term available"
rather than falling through to `unbound_local` (`T0021`).
`opaque_bindings` mirrors `maybe_bindings`'s full lifecycle, including
the solver's six control-flow join sites in
`crates/pycc_types/src/solver.rs` (`If`, `While`, `ForRange`,
`ForList`, per-`case` `Match`, `Try`).

Five commits, in order:

1. `bbb6b79d` — the base `opaque_bindings` field, its `HirExpr::Name`
   lookup wiring, and the two production construction sites.
2. `86f7d597` — propagate `opaque_bindings` through the solver's join
   sites (both loops in `join_if_branches_solver`, plus
   `join_loop_body_solver`).
3. `ca7caefe` — fix `pre_existing`'s blindness to `opaque_bindings` at
   all six call sites, plus a guard in `join_if_branches_solver`'s
   real-binding merge loops to stop them unmasking a one-branch-only
   term for a pre-existing-opaque name.
4. `0a9f0985` — narrow that guard: the round-3 form was too broad and
   incorrectly dropped a pre-existing-opaque name reassigned to a real
   term in *both* branches. Corrected to consult the other branch's
   own `bindings` map rather than the outer environment's own
   mutation state.
5. `8362862c` — no logic change. A fourth reviewer pass found the
   round-4 (numbered "third reviewer pass" in the code) fix correct,
   but caught that its own comments/D-199 prose mischaracterized the
   bug it fixed (described it as reproducing `unbound_local`; the
   actual effect was a silent masking of both branches' real terms
   back down to "opaque," since the stale pre-existing opaque marker
   was never removed by the join). Corrected the narrative in
   `solver.rs`, `tests.rs`, and D-199, and added an explicit assertion
   pinning that `env.opaque_bindings` retains a harmless stale
   duplicate marker in the both-branches case.

Three composed regression tests in `crates/pycc_types/src/tests.rs`
(`solver_if_reassigns_pre_existing_opaque_binding_in_one_branch_only`,
its orelse-branch mirror, and `..._in_both_branches_to_real_terms`)
pin all three pre-existing-opaque-then-branch-reassignment
combinations, in addition to the tests added for the base
`opaque_bindings` mechanism and its `join_loop_body_solver` mirroring.

`docs/decisions/D-199-opaque-bindings-for-solver-unrepresentable.md`

## Why this took four reviewer rounds

The original plan (produced via `issue-to-plan` before this session)
called for "mirror `maybe_bindings`'s lifecycle exactly, including the
solver's join sites" as one line item. In practice the join-site
mirroring was the hardest part to get right: three of the four
`ievo:deep-reviewer` passes each found a genuine, distinct
correctness gap in the same ~15 lines of `solver.rs` before the fourth
pass came back clean (only doc-accuracy findings, no further logic
issues). This is disclosed here and in the PR body as a deliberate
transparency measure, not hidden as if the branch had gone smoothly on
the first attempt — each gap was empirically confirmed with a targeted
test before being fixed, and the join-site logic that shipped is
covered by tests for all four semantically distinct combinations a
pre-existing-opaque name can hit across an `if`/`else` (untouched,
body-only-real, orelse-only-real, both-branches-real).
