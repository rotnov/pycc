## D-174: Gate the D-084 throughput floor on the minimum of 5 samples, superseding D-132

- Status: accepted
- Context: `build-test-coverage` run 32118069097 failed D-079/D-084's `pycc check` absolute-throughput-floor step with `FAIL: exceeded 75.0ms threshold (median of 3 runs: [58.90, 81.08, 84.84])`. A controlled comparison settles the cause: run 32150994456, on `main` commit `675dbe67`, passed the same step. `675dbe67` is a docs-only commit on top of `782d640`, so the compiler source under measurement is byte-identical between the failing and passing runs. This is a measurement-robustness defect in the gate, not a compiler regression, and specifically not a regression in the pull request that first hit it. D-132's own closing line anticipated a recurrence and suggested a remedy -- "escalate the sample count or revisit CPU-time measurement citing fresh evidence" -- but the fresh evidence refutes the first branch of that suggestion. The failing batch's **fastest** sample, 58.90ms, was already roughly twice the top of D-132's own calibrated 15-32ms operating band (~209 sampled CI runs, 2026-08-01 through 2026-08-03). All three draws were elevated together: the runner was contended for the whole batch, not for one draw. A median cancels a single contaminated draw and does nothing against a shifted distribution, so median-of-5 or median-of-7 would very likely have failed on that runner too. D-132's estimator, not its sample count, is what needs to change.
- Decision: change `measure_and_check` in `scripts/check_frontend_throughput.rb` to gate on `elapsed_times.min` rather than the median, with `samples:` defaulting to 5, and relax the sample-count guard from "positive odd integer" to "positive integer" -- oddness existed only so the median was a single element rather than an average of two, and a minimum is well defined for any positive count. Runner interference is strictly additive: co-tenant CPU steal, scheduler preemption, and frequency throttling can only make an invocation take longer than the compiler's own cost, never shorter. D-140 established this same mechanism for the structurally similar nbody gate, noting that on a virtualized hosted runner steal time is counted as full-rate CPU-seconds and therefore leaks into wall-clock and CPU-time alike. Under that additive model every sample is bounded below by the compiler's fastest achievable cost, so the minimum is too -- and that is exactly the quantity an absolute floor is meant to bound. If the fastest achievable cost exceeds 75ms then every sample exceeds 75ms and the minimum still trips, so the estimator discards inflation without being able to mask a real violation. The median has neither property: it is neither an upper nor a lower bound on the truth, and it degrades as soon as more than half the batch is contended. This argument rests on the true cost being near-constant across samples, which holds here because `tests/fixtures/pr6_1000_loc_bench.py` is fixed and deterministic -- `pycc check` performs identical work on every invocation and the only variation between samples is environmental. The 75ms absolute floor does not move, and `.github/workflows/ci.yml` is unchanged: the CLI signature stays `<pycc_bin> <fixture_path> [threshold_ms]`, so the existing invocation and `scripts/check_roadmap_evidence.rb`'s `check-throughput-1k-loc-75ms` evidence binding both keep working untouched. Keeping `ci.yml` out of the diff also keeps this a single-pull-request change, since `ci.yml` is D-103 policy-successor-manifest-protected while neither `scripts/check_frontend_throughput.rb` nor its self-test is. This entry supersedes D-132, whose front-matter and body status are updated to `superseded` without altering its reasoning text.
- Alternatives: escalate to median-of-5 or median-of-7 (rejected -- this is D-132's own suggested remedy and the fresh evidence refutes it, since the failing batch was uniformly elevated rather than singly contaminated, so a larger median would very likely have failed on that runner too); retry the whole batch once on failure (rejected -- it adds a second failure mode in which a genuine regression takes twice the samples to report, complicates the checker's control flow, and is unnecessary, since a Monte-Carlo comparison under the additive-contention model calibrated against the observed band puts min-of-5's false-failure rate at 0.06% for a compliant compiler under heavy contention, against median-of-3's 13.92%, while both retain 100% detection of a compiler whose true cost genuinely exceeds the floor); discard the first sample as an explicit warm-up and then take the median (rejected -- min-of-N already subsumes it, because a cold first sample simply never wins the minimum, at strictly lower complexity); switch to CPU-time (`rusage`) measurement (rejected -- reaffirming D-132's rejection, which rests on D-126/D-129's measurements on the nbody gate showing CPU-time did not reduce variance on Ubuntu x86_64 and made it worse on Windows; this question is answered and is not reopened here); raise or lower the 75ms threshold (rejected -- the floor is the contract this gate exists to enforce, and moving it would either mask a future genuine regression or weaken an acceptance bar in response to noise, which is the same reasoning D-132 used to decline a threshold change and D-140 used to decline lowering nbody's 20x floor).
- Consequences: the checker now invokes `pycc check` 5 times per CI run instead of 3, roughly two extra 15-30ms invocations, which is negligible beside this job's multi-minute build-and-coverage steps. The gate becomes substantially harder to trip by runner contention and no easier to pass with a genuinely slow frontend, because the minimum remains bounded below by the compiler's own cost. One honest bias is accepted: preferring the fastest sample also prefers the warmest filesystem and page-cache state. This is not new relative to median-of-3 -- samples 2..N are identically warm under both designs -- and it is aligned with D-084's stated intent, which positions this step after `cargo test` in `ci.yml` precisely so the measurement reflects steady-state frontend throughput rather than one-time cold-start or code-signature-validation overhead. This entry must not be cited as precedent for gates whose noise is bidirectional: anything comparing two measurements against each other, such as `frontend-perf-gate`'s Criterion harness or nbody's ratio of two medians, is a different problem, and the additive-noise argument that licenses a minimum here does not transfer to it. Two related gaps are deliberately left open for a follow-up change, both blocked behind D-103's two-merge stage-then-activate process: `ruby scripts/test_check_frontend_throughput.rb` is not invoked by any workflow, so this checker's self-test is not a merge gate; and `scripts/classify_ci_changes.py` binds `check_replicated_paired_perf_regression.rb` together with its self-test but binds `check_frontend_throughput.rb` without its self-test, so a pull request touching only that self-test selects no heavy gate at all.
