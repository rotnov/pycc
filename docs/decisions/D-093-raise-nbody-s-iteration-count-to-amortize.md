## D-093: Raise nbody's iteration count to amortize benchmark overhead; ratio still short of the 20x gate

- Status: accepted
- Context: Task 5's first pass at `tests/nbody_bench.rs` (D-094's harness) measured a ~10-11x speedup ratio at pyperformance's own `DEFAULT_ITERATIONS = 20000`, far short of the design spec's (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-07-28-v0-2-collections-generics-design.md` §1) required ≥20x gate. A second-reviewer pass (via the task dispatcher) re-derived the root cause from the first report's own numbers: CPython's nbody total (68.2ms) minus its own baseline (`python3.14 -c pass`, 20.3ms) gives a compute-only figure of ~47.9ms; pycc's nbody total (6.1ms) minus its own trivial-binary baseline (3.0ms) gives ~3.1ms — a compute-only ratio of ~15.5x already, well above the measured 11.2x. The gap was fixed per-process overhead eating a much larger fraction of pycc's own tiny ~6ms measurement (~45-50%) than of CPython's ~68ms measurement (~29%): a 6ms workload cannot support whole-process wall-clock timing as a clean compute proxy. This is the same class of mistake `docs/AGENT_RETROSPECTIVE.md` already logged for Task 3 (binary-size-as-optimization-proxy) — a measurement with near-zero signal for what it's meant to measure — occurring a second time in this one PR. Separately, `--release` was found to always link a debug `pycc_rt` regardless of the flag — a real bug, fixed and recorded as its own decision, D-092, not repeated here.
- Decision: scale `tests/fixtures/nbody.py`'s `iterations` from `20000` to `525000`, chosen empirically (not by linear extrapolation, which real measurement showed was unreliable here) after scanning safe candidates and directly timing several: at `525000`, pycc's own fixed overhead is ~3.0ms of a ~70.3ms total (~4.3%) and CPython's is ~20.3ms of a ~1273ms total (~1.6%) — both comfortably single-digit, and CPython's own median (~1.27s) stays well under the "couple of seconds" ceiling appropriate for a benchmark that spawns 10 subprocesses per Tier-1 leg on every PR. Re-verified byte-for-byte identical output between CPython 3.14.6 and pycc (debug and `--release`) at the new count: `0.008420846407813569 0.003871558303952385 -0.00032661880191911866`. The iteration count is not a freely tunable knob: a direct simulation scan across candidate counts found the "does this stay in `pycc_rt_float_to_str`'s supported `1e-4 <= |x| < 1e16` non-scientific-notation range" property is **non-monotonic** in the iteration count — `20000`/`30000`/`40000`/`60000`/`70000`/`90000`/`120000`/`150000`/`200000`/`525000` are safe, but `50000`/`80000`/`100000`/`275000`/`325000`/`450000`/`500000`/`550000`/`600000` are not (the sun's position from the fixed reference frame chaotically approaches near-zero magnitude at various points, and pycc's float formatter panics rather than falling back to scientific notation — a known, documented v0.1 gap). Anyone changing this count later must re-run the same direct-simulation safety scan, not assume a "larger is safer" pattern.
- Measured outcome: even with D-092's fix applied too, five consecutive `cargo test --test nbody_bench -- --ignored` runs at `iterations = 525000` measured ratios of 18.04x, 18.06x, 18.17x, 18.24x, 18.04x (CPython median stable at 1.273-1.277s, pycc `--release` median stable at 0.0698-0.0706s) — a genuinely reproducible, empirically-bounded plateau (300k→17.34x, 400k→17.72x, 525k→17.78x, 800k→18.03x, 1M→18.14x measured directly, flat across a 3.3x iteration range, not still climbing toward 20x), not a measurement artifact. This is short of the design spec's 20x gate. Per this task's own instructions, the gate is a spec requirement, not a knob to adjust to force a pass, and the shortfall is not resolved by further raising the iteration count (the plateau is the asymptotic compute-only ratio, which further overhead amortization cannot exceed). Reported to the task dispatcher as BLOCKED with the full measurement breakdown; whether to invest further (e.g. real cross-module optimization for `pycc_rt_float_pow`) or revise the gate/methodology is reserved for that decision-maker, not decided unilaterally here.
- Alternatives: keep interleaving pycc/CPython runs instead of the existing block-then-block order (considered, not changed — the design spec's own wording specifies `K = 5` runs each and the median of each, not an interleaving requirement, and the block order's own exposure to monotonic drift is already blunted by using the median rather than the mean of 5 same-block runs). Rewrite the fixture's `**` calls to avoid `pycc_rt_float_pow` entirely, chasing the ratio (rejected outright — this is benchmark-shopping against the actual computation the design spec asks to measure, not a legitimate optimization). Push the iteration count far higher (e.g. 1,000,000+) to approach the ~18.3x asymptote more closely (rejected for this change — empirically, 800k/1M measurements already show the ratio is not still climbing meaningfully, so the added CPython wall-clock cost across every Tier-1 leg would not be worth the marginal signal; revisit only if the dispatcher decides the exact asymptote value itself needs pinning down further).
- Consequences: `tests/nbody_bench.rs`'s `#[ignore]`d test still fails (by design, honestly) until either the compute-only ceiling is raised (real cross-module optimization work, out of this task's scope) or the gate/fixture is revisited by the dispatcher. **Update:** D-095 later revisited the gate, once all five Tier-1 legs' real numbers existed -- it does not change this decision's own history or its 20x figure, but establishes a documented, evidence-backed exception (12x) for macOS aarch64 specifically, while this decision's 20x stands unmodified everywhere else.
