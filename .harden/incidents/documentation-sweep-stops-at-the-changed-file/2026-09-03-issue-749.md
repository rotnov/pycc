doc-drift rows, one class. No round 2 was needed.

## What happened

Issue #749 deleted the never-registered PEP 681 fixture and retargeted the
stale "tracked as #248" pointer for the PEP 681 divergence to D-196/#749.
The plan (issue comment, item 5) scoped that retarget with a directory-bounded
search — "this makes `grep -rn "#248" crates/` return nothing" — and named
one comment site, `crates/pycc_types/src/tests.rs`. The implementation commit
(`582b6259`) executed the plan faithfully. Two more comments attributing the
same divergence to #248 lived outside the plan's search boundary, under
`tests/`: the `tests/fixtures/pep_3129_class_deco.py` header (which also
claimed PEP 681 "keeps its own fixture" after the diff deleted it) and the
doc comment on the PEP 3129 conformance test at
`tests/conformance/classes.rs:297`. Round 1 of the pinned review found both;
the fix round (`39f02a03`) ran the tree-wide search step 5 prescribes,
adjudicated every hit, recorded the search and its hit count in the `note`,
and the next round reported nothing. Cost: one review round, two
comment-only edits.

## Why it was not caught

The variant is one phase earlier than the four previous entries: the extent
was fixed by the *plan's* search scope, not by the reviewer's cited sites.
`issue-to-plan`'s affected-site inventory rule binds "an exceptional case
added to a rule a document branches on"; a stale-pointer retarget is not that
shape, so nothing at the plan fork asked for a tree-wide search, and the
implementer brief (step 4) hands the plan through as-is. **Gap type:
trigger** at the plan fork — but the downstream rungs closed it at the
lowest possible cost, which is the datum this entry exists to record.

This pile is a measurement for the pending #867 entry: its Proof asked for
"every `fixed` doc-drift row's `note` names its search and hit count" and
"no round-N+1 finding of the shape sweep incomplete". Both hold here — the
fix-round sentence fired and produced a complete sweep in one round. It is
one positive observation, not a verdict flip; the #729 entry measured the
same sentence at zero on the narrow-spelling sub-class, and the two
observations are about different halves of the sentence (tree-wide extent
versus spelling coverage).

## Artefact and why this type

Build nothing. The ladder, rung by rung:

- **Static:** not decidable. Which `#248` mentions are stale is semantic —
  `docs/ROADMAP.md:183` keeps one on purpose as dated narrative, and the
  breadth checker, its test, the manifest, and `PYTHON_STANDARDS.md:37` are
  legitimately about #248's own topic. The topic's original `precommit`
  proposal (`incident.md`, path/symbol reference integrity) stays pending and
  would not fire here either: `#248` is a closed issue, not a path or symbol.
- **Textual (plan-template or implementer-brief sentence):** disqualified.
  Seven files in this topic, and the #729 entry already measured a textual
  rung in this topic at zero; the skill's own rule is that a text that has
  failed three times fails a fourth.
- **Seam:** no tool-call fact distinguishes a plan-scoped search from a
  tree-wide one.
- **Review:** caught every member, and the step-5 fix-round rule then
  bounded the cost to one round. This is the rung the class is at, and it
  worked.

If the plan-phase variant recurs, the next rung is mechanical, not text: a
diff-scoped retarget check — when a commit's removed lines drop an issue or
decision reference (`#NNN`, `D-NNN`) that its added lines replace with
another, list every remaining tracked occurrence of the dropped reference
outside the dated historical records and require each to be adjudicated in
the commit or plan. Same shape as `incident.md`'s pending gate, applied to
references rather than paths. Recorded here; not built, for the same reason
every entry in this topic gives — it is its own change with its own review
and a repository-wide sweep before it can be enabled.
