# Incident: paraphrase-of-a-formal-rule-drifts-from-its-source (first file)

**Batch:** `.harden/findings/issue-868.jsonl`, rows 12 (round 4), 13-14
(round 5), 15-16 (round 6): five `fixed` doc-drift rows, one class, three
consecutive review rounds on one sentence.

## What happened

D-220's Decision section states rule 4 (the solver-first merge) formally, in
terms of two keyed lists. Seven other sites — `check_all`'s doc comment,
`src/frontend.rs`, `docs/CLI_SPEC.md`, `docs/DIAGNOSTICS.md`,
`docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`, the `pycc` skill, and D-220's own Consequences
bullet — each restated it in prose. Round 4 found the restatement wrong (a
two-way split where the merge has three arms); the fix answered that
counter-example. Round 5 found the third arm missing; the fix added it.
Round 6 found the "solver flags functions" arm still too narrow (it named
only the checker's module-level entry, not its per-function entries) and one
fabricated rationale ("a partial or inconsistent environment") in
`check_all`'s doc. The same finding had now survived two genuine fix
attempts — the step-5 stop rule. The third attempt was not identical: one
canonical sentence derived from the Decision rule and from
`merge_solver_first`'s code was installed at all seven sites and verified by
the orchestrating session's own diff read against that code, and the loop was
closed there instead of by a seventh reviewer round.

## Why it was not caught

**Gap type: absence.** No rule says that a formally stated rule is quoted or
cross-referenced rather than paraphrased, nor that a paraphrase is verified
against the rule's source instead of against the counter-example that
prompted the edit. Each fix round did the natural thing — patched the arm the
reviewer showed — and left every other arm as imprecise as before. Not
`own-change-falsifies-adjacent-prose` (existing prose falsified by a change:
this prose was new, written by the change about itself) and not
`doc-comment-overclaims-unqualified-scope` (a scope word missing: here whole
arms of a rule were missing or invented).

## Artefact and why this type

A project rule in `AGENTS.md`'s "Keep documentation current" section: when a
canonical statement exists, other documents quote or cross-reference it; a
restatement that must exist is checked against the canonical statement and
its implementing code, never against the prompting counter-example. Rule
tier because the failure is a choice made while writing documentation (which
words to use), it recurs at every site of every future rule, and it binds
every harness through the governance file; no static command can compare a
prose paraphrase to a numbered rule. What detects the class is reading the
paraphrase against the code — review tier — and the rule's job is to remove
the paraphrase, not to catch it.

## Proof

with a numbered rule: its other sites cite or quote the rule, and no review
round reports a paraphrase arm missing. A second file in this topic means the
rule did not reach the writing step and the next rung is a pointer inside the
